# Viral moment detection (v0.8.1+): how it works and how it did

## Why moments were wrong before (root causes, from step 1)
- **The AI rating took the maximum of "funny", "hype" and "stop-scroll".** Anything emotional scored high, including a sad story.
- **Condolence chat counted as hype.** Messages like "F", "o7" and "Sadge" during a sad moment looked like a hype spike.
- **The sound model's labels were mapped wrongly.** YAMNet's "Crowd", engine/driving and "Burst/Bang/Thump" counted as excitement, so calm driving and street noise looked like action.
- **Typing fell back to "action".** With no clear type, moments were typed as action and ranked accordingly.
- **Camera shake alone counted as a crash** (fixed in v0.8.3). Scene cuts and facecam movement produced "Crash at 0:00".

## What it learns from (all permitted, nothing from TikTok or Instagram)

| Source | What | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Twitch Get Clips, by game, last 24 h / 7 days, permitted streamers only | The window each top community clip covers, with features extracted from the VOD | viral (weighted by views) |
| The same VODs | Windows at least 60 s away from any clip, plus mundane stretches (menus, driving, talking) | not viral |
| YouTube Shorts (official API), permitted channels | Titles and event words, which feed the trending-tags context | context |
| Your posts | Views after 24–48 h on the clips you posted | viral / not, by your channel's median |
| Your 👍 / 👎 and tags | "great moment", "boring game part", … | viral / not |
| Your style references | Tags on your screenshots (fail, rage, …) | trending-tags context |

- **Refresh:** daily, as a background job. A retrain runs automatically after each refresh.
- **Settings › Highlights › Viral moment model** shows:
  - the dataset size per game;
  - each game's accuracy (hold-out AUC and top-10 precision against the starting model);
  - when it was last trained;
  - a Reset button.

## Features per moment
- **Reaction (weighted heaviest):**
  - laughter;
  - screams;
  - disbelief words ("no way", "bro", "what");
  - a sudden jump in voice level;
  - facecam motion.
- **Chat:**
  - rate compared with that channel's normal;
  - laughing or hype emotes;
  - "clip it".
- **What happened:** event tags per second (crash, explosion, gunfire, police, death, stunt, fall, NPC, vehicle steal, plane, boat, chase, fail, easter egg, money). These come from sounds, visuals, the streamer's words and chat. An optional local vision model can add more.
- **Surprise:** an event followed by a reaction within about 3 s.
- **LLM rating:** funny / chaotic / shocking minus serious. Sad or serious talk counts against a moment.
- **Context:**
  - community clips nearby;
  - this week's trending event tags for the game;
  - interactions, e.g. explosion × laughter and crash × scream.

## Model
- **Type:** a per-game logistic model.
  - It starts from research-based default weights.
  - It's trained with a pull back toward those defaults, so a little data can't wreck it.
  - Below 40 examples it keeps the defaults.
- **Hard penalties:**
  - menus or loading screens;
  - BRB;
  - cutscenes with no reaction;
  - calm talk;
  - sad or serious talk;
  - calm driving.
- **Output:**
  - **score:** 0–100;
  - **type:** Funny, Chaos/Fail, Action + Reaction, Rage, Clutch/Skill or Wholesome;
  - **event tags:** with timestamps;
  - **one-line reason;**
  - **"Why this moment?":** the top contributions, shown in the Editor.
- **Boundaries:** a clip starts 1–3 s before the setup, and a "with setup" variant is offered when the setup is longer.
- **Where the score goes:** into virality (30 % weight) and projected earnings.

## Evaluation (CI, every build)
**The labelled set:**
- 3 synthetic VODs, about 9 minutes each;
- real CC0 laughter, explosions, gunfire, cheering and screaming;
- speech from espeak-ng;
- 13 labelled hits.

**Traps that must never be picked:**
- sad talk with condolence chat;
- calm driving;
- story cutscenes;
- menus, loading and BRB screens;
- calm talking;
- shouting at chat;
- loud music;
- sub trains;
- explosions with no reaction.

Each detector runs in its own process. **v0.7.1 is the real previous release, checked out from git.**

| Detector | Top-10 precision | Recall (moments found in the top 10) | Traps in top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| v0.3 | 50 % | 38 % | 0 |
| v0.7.1 (before) | 70 % | 54 % | 1 (a sad segment) |
| **v0.8 (after)** | **90 %** | **69 %** | **0** |

These figures are from CI run 45146161411, commit 5a34263.

**The CI gate fails the build if any of these is true:**
- the after precision is below 80 %;
- it doesn't beat v0.7.1 by 15 points;
- recall drops;
- any trap appears in the top 10.

**One build failed the gate**, at 80 % vs 70 % with 1 trap. That led to the crash-tag fix in v0.8.3. The full top-10 tables for every detector are now printed in the CI log.

## Not done here: the 20 picks from a real GTA 6 VOD
This needs your Twitch connection and a permitted GTA 6 streamer, so it has to run on your PC:
1. Start a GTA 6 project with a permitted streamer, so ClipFarm has their VOD.
2. Go to **Settings › Highlights › Viral moment model › Make detection report on a real VOD**.

The report uses the newest VOD it has from a streamer you're allowed to clip. It takes 10–30 minutes.
The report goes to `media/reports/viral_report_<date>.md`. It contains:
- the dataset size;
- the hold-out accuracy (AUC) and top-10 precision, compared with the starting model;
- how many of that VOD's top-10 community clips the old ranking and the new ranking each found;
- the 20 picked moments, with times, scores, types, tags, reasons and whether each matches a community clip.
